# Side-view squat measurements

MoveIDE keeps all 33 MediaPipe landmark slots at every sampled video timestamp in AnalysisReport v1. `getMotionTrace(report)` derives a local timeline from those samples. `getSquatProfile(report)` collects bottom measurements and phase durations for complete reps and identifies their source frames. Both are derived views of the original report. [MediaPipe landmark definitions](https://developers.google.com/edge/mediapipe/solutions/vision/pose_landmarker#pose-landmarker-model)

All measurements use one selected anatomical side and the display-oriented video plane. The calculations multiply normalized horizontal and vertical coordinates by the corresponding frame dimensions before taking distances or angles. Use a stationary camera from the side with the whole lifter and the near foot in frame. Values are screen-plane estimates, with no reference joint angles in the fixture dataset.

- **Knee flexion in degrees** is 180° minus the hip–knee–ankle angle at the knee. An approximately straight knee is near 0°.
- **Torso lean in degrees** is the unsigned angle of the hip-to-shoulder segment from upward video vertical.
- **Projected hip flexion in degrees** is 180° minus the shoulder–hip–knee angle at the hip. It describes how far the visible torso and thigh fold in the image.
- **Shin inclination in degrees** is the unsigned angle of the ankle-to-knee segment from upward video vertical.
- **Projected ankle closure change in degrees** is the rep-start opening angle minus the current opening angle. Opening is the angle between the ankle-to-knee and heel-to-foot-index vectors. A positive change means the shank-to-foot angle narrowed relative to the start. A negative change means it opened. The proxy needs a visible heel and foot index at both timestamps. A foot axis under 10% of the projected shin length or under one pixel is treated as collapsed.
- **Hip descent in start-leg lengths** is the downward change in the hip's video position divided by the same-side projected hip-to-knee plus knee-to-ankle length at rep start. Positive values indicate descent from the starting position.
- **Hip above knee in start-leg lengths** is the vertical distance from the hip down to the knee, divided by the same start leg length. Positive values put the hip above the knee in the image. Zero is level, and negative values place the hip below the knee in the image.

A horizontal mirror preserves these quantities because they use segment angles, lengths and vertical differences. The chosen anatomical side or visibility can change if a mirrored video is analyzed again. The start-relative metrics have a value only inside a complete rep with usable start evidence. Frames or required landmarks that lack usable evidence yield null values. Samples keep their original timestamps, including gaps. Phase labels follow detected start, bottom and end timestamps. At the rep bottom, the measurements use the original landmarks from that frame.

Each rep profile gives descent and ascent durations as the time from start to bottom and bottom to end. Its source references identify the exact sampled frames by clip-relative timestamp and `frame-<timestamp>ms` ID. Ankle closure and normalized positions also depend on the rep start frame. The positions and angles describe the projected motion under this capture protocol. [AnalysisReport and CoachPacket contract](../../_bmad-output/specs/shared/analysis-report-v1.md)

## Model presets

The harness defaults to the pinned Heavy float16 v1 task, selected by the user. Setup stages both Heavy and Full with SHA-256 verification. Select the lighter alternative with `analyzeSquat(source, { model: 'full' })`. Heavy passes both positive fixtures within the unchanged 150 ms bottom tolerance. The earlier paired CPU benchmark found Full faster and closer to the timing labels on the supported clip, so the default change is a product choice, not a claim of measured accuracy superiority. The worker verifies the selected model's SHA-256 before inference. [Versioned Heavy task](https://storage.googleapis.com/mediapipe-models/pose_landmarker/pose_landmarker_heavy/float16/1/pose_landmarker_heavy.task) · [Local benchmark results](../results/pose-model-benchmark.json)

From the repository root, run `npm --prefix harness run setup:pose` and then `node harness/verify/pose-benchmark.mjs --write` to repeat the comparison. The benchmark downloads and verifies Heavy before starting its browser, then serves the asset through its own loopback route.

## Experimental multi-view diagnostic amendment

`analyzeMultiViewFrame(raw, {width, height, view})` in `multiview.mjs` consumes one existing `RawPoseFrame`. It returns a separate diagnostic object and **does not change AnalysisReport v1, the supported side-view squat protocol, rep detection, CoachPacket, or scoring**. `view` is a caller-declared `side`, `front`, `oblique`, or `unknown` capture view; this adapter does not verify the view or calibrate a camera. Unknown view withholds every metric.

Every metric includes `value`, `unit`, `status` (`estimated` or `unavailable`), an explicit `reason`, minimum required landmark visibility/presence, and model ID/version provenance. A missing value is `null`, never zero. Confidence is a model tracking signal, not an angle-accuracy percentage. Each required landmark must have an observed visibility score: normalized visibility for projected metrics, or at least one available normalized/world visibility score for world-derived metrics. The minimum of all available visibility and optional presence scores must be at least 0.5. Missing optional fields are omitted, never replaced with 1.0; presence alone cannot replace missing visibility. World-derived metrics also require the corresponding normalized landmarks to remain visible inside the image. Missing world coordinates are not replaced with normalized image Z.

| Diagnostic, for left and right sides | Definition | Availability |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `KneeFlexion3d` | 180° minus the estimated world hip–knee–ankle included angle | Declared side/front/oblique view with all required world/image evidence |
| `HipFlexion3d` | 180° minus the estimated world shoulder–hip–knee included angle | Same evidence rules; this is a torso–thigh folding proxy, not calibrated anatomical hip rotation |
| `KneeMedialOffsetLegLengths` | Signed perpendicular distance from the projected knee to the projected hip–ankle line, divided by hip–knee plus knee–ankle projected length | Front view only, with both hips and the measured leg visible |
| `KneeMedialOffsetHipSpans` | The same signed projected distance divided by projected inter-hip distance | Front view only, with a non-collapsed hip span |

Metric keys prepend `left` or `right`, for example `leftKneeFlexion3d`. Angles use degrees; offsets use `projected_leg_lengths` or `projected_hip_spans`. Positive offsets mean toward the opposite hip, negative means away, and zero means on the hip–ankle line. This definition preserves the sign under horizontal image mirroring without assuming the person's anatomical left is the left side of the screen. Normalized image X/Y are multiplied by image width/height before geometry, so non-square frames are handled correctly. A hip span below one pixel or 5% of projected leg length is withheld; a hip direction nearly collinear with the hip–ankle axis is also withheld because inward direction becomes ambiguous.

The world-angle calculation is invariant to rigid coordinate rotation, translation, reflection and uniform scale, within numerical limits. **That mathematical property does not establish view-independent MediaPipe accuracy.** Its XYZ coordinates are monocular model estimates, not measured calibrated 3D motion capture. Changes in view, occlusion, clothing and pose can still change the estimated landmarks and thus the reported angles. There are no reference multi-view or ground-truth joint-angle benchmarks for this adapter yet. [MediaPipe output coordinate definitions](https://developers.google.com/edge/mediapipe/solutions/vision/pose_landmarker#pose-landmarker-model)

Frontal inward-offset values are **withheld for oblique footage** with reason `oblique_uncalibrated`, and for side footage with `front_view_required`. An oblique image does not receive a clinical valgus estimate or a front-view correction inferred from model Z. The adapter has no calibrated projection path. These front offsets are descriptive screen-plane proxies, not knee-abduction angles, diagnoses, injury-risk scores or universal technique fault thresholds. No automatic good/bad classification should be built from their sign alone.

All 33 raw slots remain available elsewhere, but this adapter estimates only the listed shoulder/hip/knee/ankle relationships. It does not infer spinal curvature, core engagement, muscle forces, loads, calibrated segment lengths, or full-body biomechanical validity. Existing side-view metrics stay the supported product path; these values are explicitly experimental extensions until reviewed against independent annotated front/oblique data.

Run `node --test harness/motion/multiview.test.mjs` for known synthetic angles and offsets, rotated/scaled/reflected world geometry, mirrored/non-square image geometry, missing world data, missing/low confidence, cropping, degenerate segments, unknown views and oblique withholding. These tests verify the formulas and abstention behavior; they are not a model-accuracy study.

### Actual SDK confidence verification

`node harness/motion/multiview.verify-browser.mjs` runs pinned Heavy on the existing clear-squat fixture in Chrome and records only aggregate confidence-field counts and metric availability in [multiview-browser-evidence.json](multiview-browser-evidence.json). It uses no camera or external model/API requests. This explicit browser verification is separate from the fast geometry tests.

The 2026-11-07 run observed 57 raw frames (810 × 1440). All 1,881 normalized and all 1,881 world landmarks supplied both visibility and presence in the pinned SDK. Both before and after optional-field handling was made more tolerant, left knee and hip diagnostics were available on 57/57 frames; the occluded right side was withheld on 57/57 for low confidence. Front offsets were withheld throughout because the declared fixture view is side. Thus missing optional fields did not break this installed model, while synthetic regression tests cover SDK outputs where those fields are absent. These availability counts validate integration and abstention, not physical angle accuracy.
