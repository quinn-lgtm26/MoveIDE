# Side-view squat measurement and review

## What the first protocol can report

Record one lifter from a level, stationary, side-on camera near hip height. Keep the visible shoulder, hip, knee, ankle, and whole foot in frame throughout. Start and end standing, use adequate lighting, and record comparable reps with the same viewpoint, stance, load, and footwear. Save the intended depth or tempo as a user goal. A front or oblique view, occlusion, or a changing crop can make the projected angles unsuitable for comparison. [Google Pose Landmarker documentation](https://developers.google.com/edge/mediapipe/solutions/vision/pose_landmarker?tab=Web) · [Example of a standardized sagittal-plane video setup](https://www.mdpi.com/2673-7078/6/1/1)

- **Knee flexion and range.** Use the visible hip, knee, and ankle on one side in aspect-correct video coordinates. Measure 180° minus the included angle at the knee. Report the value at the lowest tracked hip frame and optionally the within-rep range across usable samples. Label the true maximum separately if it falls at another timestamp. If a coach wants a geometric depth target, a visible thigh’s angle to the image horizontal is a separate candidate measure for a level camera. It requires a separately defined and validated threshold.
- **Projected trunk lean.** Measure the same-side shoulder-to-hip line relative to image vertical, at the same bottom timestamp. This describes how the visible torso inclines in the video. Compare it within the same lifter, exercise variation, and capture setup. Leave sagittal ankle/hip angles for later validation because they add foot or pelvis landmark requirements. [Sagittal squat kinematics study](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7429430/) · [Effects of stance width, foot angle and experience](https://doi.org/10.1186/s13102-018-0103-7)
- **Timing and consistency.** Report complete rep count, start, bottom, return, and total duration. Derive descent and ascent duration from those events and show the sample times in playback. Define a measurable bottom interval before reporting pause duration. Compare equivalent phases and show angle, duration, and coverage differences. Use multiple same-setup reps to form a personal baseline.
- **Measurement quality.** Distinguish complete reps from partial reps and usable frames from insufficient evidence. Surface clipping, occlusion, and low visibility. Use the original clip for visual observations such as an apparent near-foot heel rise. Leave pressure, forces, joint moments, knee valgus, bilateral symmetry, and clinical interpretation outside the side-view protocol.

## Interpreting the numbers

A quality rep in MoveIDE completes the intended motion and gives the coach usable footage to judge the chosen depth and tempo. Compare against the lifter’s stated goal, a coach-defined reference, or a matched personal baseline. When no goal is given, describe measurable differences and ask for context through the coaching flow. The current timing test uses two manually annotated bottoms and a ±150 ms acceptance limit. Establish independent angle annotation and repeated-capture variability before interpreting small angle differences as meaningful.

As study context, Cotter and colleagues measured mean peak knee flexion of approximately 99°, 124°, and 141° for their above-parallel, parallel, and below-parallel back-squat conditions across three loads in 16 trained men. Their protocol used specific geometric cues, laboratory instrumentation, and loaded as well as unloaded trials. These values illustrate how the specified depth changes the angle. Use the selected goal and recorded geometry for product feedback. Lorenzetti and colleagues also varied stance width, foot angle, and experience, and measured differences in squat mechanics. [Cotter et al., Table 1](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4064719/) · [Lorenzetti et al.](https://doi.org/10.1186/s13102-018-0103-7)

## Capture, processing, and playback

The current worker analyzes a 15 Hz target grid, records each decoded source presentation timestamp, and drops duplicate timestamps. The six-second supported regression fixture yields 90 analysis samples. The clearer 3.8-second handoff fixture yields 57 samples, with two detected bottoms at 1,000 and 2,933 ms. Errors against the independent labels are 67 and 0 ms. An exact source-frame mode for higher-rate or variable-rate files is separate work. Evaluate local sequential decode with MP4 demuxing and WebCodecs for supported codecs. Keep source timestamps, decoded pixel orientation, and dimensions associated with each frame. Use increasing presentation time in MediaPipe VIDEO mode. Report how many frames were decoded and analyzed, any gaps, and cancellation. [Clearer fixture labels](../../../fixtures/squat/clear-squat.labels.json) · [Google Pose Landmarker video API](https://developers.google.com/edge/mediapipe/solutions/vision/pose_landmarker?tab=Web) · [W3C WebCodecs Working Draft](https://www.w3.org/TR/webcodecs/)

Preserve raw samples and mark gaps. Use a documented, versioned smoother for phase segmentation if needed. A displayed measurement and its source image must resolve to the same unsmoothed frame and landmark coordinates. Sync the overlay to presented media time through `requestVideoFrameCallback` and the displayed content rectangle. Recompute on seek and resize, preserve the 100 ms overlay gap rule from the shared contract, and expose stepping through analyzed samples, slow playback, and phase/evidence jumps. Compare two reps at the same named phase while displaying each rep’s original timestamp and duration. The frame callback describes video presented for composition, so use explicit local decode when every source frame matters. [Video frame callback specification](https://wicg.github.io/video-rvfc/) · [AnalysisReport v1 contract](../shared/analysis-report-v1.md)

For AI-assisted review, keep the full video and trace local. Send the approved packet and reviewed JPEGs on request. Playback continues independently while the provider processes the request. Render a completed observation as a seekable cue by resolving its submitted measurement or frame ID to the source timestamp. Reject unknown IDs and ignore results after a clip or rep change. ## Verification and OpenCap research

Keep count/timing labels independent of model output. Add two independent angle annotators with adjudication on visible 2D landmarks and compare at identical source timestamps. Report coverage, absolute angle error, bias, agreement, and rep timing error separately. Evaluate clear and hard captures, repeat captures from the same setup, rotated and variable-rate sources, and near/far-side occlusion. When testing 3D, use synchronized reference motion capture and separately name the coordinate definition and error metrics.

**MediaPipe variants.** As checked on November 7, 2026, the stable Web SDK is 1.2.2 and the official model registry exposes Full and Heavy float16 assets under `/1` and `/latest`. The two Full archives have different outer checksums and identical embedded detector and landmark model hashes. Heavy `/1` and `/latest` have the same content hash. Compare the pinned Full and Heavy v1 assets on the same decoded timestamps, CPU, thresholds, and fixture labels. Include a clearer redistributable side view with independently labeled complete reps, plus the current positive, occluded, and wrong-view fixtures. Record timing, annotated angle error where available, coverage, runtime, asset size, and memory. Promote Heavy only if the measured improvement justifies the additional on-device cost. Confirm asset-specific redistribution terms before bundling Heavy. [Google model catalog](https://developers.google.com/edge/mediapipe/solutions/vision/pose_landmarker?tab=Web#models) · [Full asset registry](https://storage.googleapis.com/storage/v1/b/mediapipe-models/o?prefix=pose_landmarker%2Fpose_landmarker_full%2Ffloat16%2F) · [Heavy asset registry](https://storage.googleapis.com/storage/v1/b/mediapipe-models/o?prefix=pose_landmarker%2Fpose_landmarker_heavy%2Ffloat16%2F) · [Web SDK registry](https://registry.npmjs.org/@mediapipe%2ftasks-vision/latest)

The first paired Chromium CPU benchmark ran one cold worker per model and clip on an Apple M4 Pro. On the supported sample, both models produced two reps and 90 usable frames out of 90. Full matched the two labels exactly in 3.77 s. Heavy’s bottom errors were 0 and 67 ms in 7.75 s. On the hard sample, Full had 27 usable frames out of 53 and Heavy had 28. Both withheld rep counts. Full remains the default. The benchmark measures timing and coverage on these clips. Independent angle references and repeated timing runs are the next evidence needed for a broader comparison. [Browser model benchmark](../../../harness/results/pose-model-benchmark.json)

**OpenCap Monocular.** The official repository had no tagged release on November 7, 2026. Its `main` head was `7c5c1dd` from November 6. The preprint validated against synchronized reference recordings from ten healthy adults, using a fixed 45° anterolateral smartphone view. It reports 4.8° mean absolute rotational error across 18 degrees of freedom and all tested tasks, including squatting, and 3.4 cm pelvic translation error. These are pooled validation results for a different protocol. The authors report processing a 10-second clip in under two minutes on an RTX 4090. The repository’s installation guide lists Ubuntu, Python 3.9, and NVIDIA CUDA among the tested configurations. [OpenCap Monocular preprint](https://arxiv.org/html/2603.24733v1) · [Pinned repository commit](https://github.com/utahmobl/opencap-monocular/commit/7c5c1dd459835e2482127e55dc45e98d68a1a9f2) · [Installation guide](https://github.com/utahmobl/opencap-monocular/blob/7c5c1dd459835e2482127e55dc45e98d68a1a9f2/installation/INSTALL_SLIM.md)
